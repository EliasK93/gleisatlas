# Day history

## 1 Overview

Day-level history: a daily time series with a 28-day trend in place of the monthly one (months stay as a switch), and a day mode of the map that colours every station by one past day against its normal for that weekday. Both rest on a per-day delay histogram. The planned feature direction is in SPEC §3.4; Bundesländer, a calendar view and marked events come later.

**Coverage.** The day views reach the last complete day, about one to two days back: the days after the last monthly file are built from the source's raw responses and refreshed by `pipeline.update` (mechanics and the completeness rule: [pipeline.md](pipeline.md) §2, "Raw days"). The month views (heatmap, rankings, monthly series, station sparkline) stay on whole months. When a month's file arrives, it replaces that month's raw days.

## 2 Spec

**Daily time series** (`/muster/zeitverlauf`, nationwide or one station):

- One point per calendar day of the data: punctuality, cancellation rate, median and P90, each with `n`. A faint line shows the daily values, a bold line the trend.
- Days with missing source hours are drawn dashed and listed in the tooltip. They still show their own values but never feed the trend. The same goes for days with little data: a day feeds a metric's trend only when its value would be shown, by the same rule as the dashed days.
- Timetable changes stay marked. Days are the default view; a Tag/Monat switch returns to the monthly series. Line series and the station sparkline stay monthly.
- The table mode lists the days.

**Day map** (`/karte?tag=YYYY-MM-DD`):

- **Switch and picker.** The map's filter bar starts with a "Zeitraum | Tag" switch. "Tag" selects the last day of the data and replaces the period chip with a native date field limited to the first and last day of `/datenstand`. "Zeitraum" returns to the period map. A day in the URL outside the data moves to the nearest edge, an impossible date is dropped, and `von` and `bis` next to a `tag` are dropped, so the API is never asked for a day it has no data for. The date field writes a day to the URL only when it is complete and inside the data, so typing a year key by key neither moves the map nor queries the API. The key is described in [app-shell.md](app-shell.md) §2.
- **Colour.** Each station is coloured by the step of its API `klasse` for the selected metric, on a five-step diverging scale from "viel besser" through "wie üblich" to "viel schlechter". The metric switch picks the metric. The API computes the step from z, the deviation of the day's value from the station's normal divided by the spread of the station's own reference days (§4). The sign is chosen so that better is positive. |z| below 2 is "wie üblich", 2 up to 4 is "besser" or "schlechter", 4 and more is "viel besser" or "viel schlechter". The frontend only maps the step to a colour.
- **Grey "kein Vergleich möglich".** A station is grey where there is nothing to compare: its value is not shown (the little-data rules of the period map apply to the day's `n`), it has only cancellations, or it has no normal. Grey dots draw below the coloured ones. The pale and very pale rules of the period map apply to the day's `n` too.
- **Legend.** The five step names under a diverging bar, the sentence "„Wie üblich“ heißt: innerhalb der gewohnten Schwankung der Station an diesem Wochentag" (no numbers: the limits differ per station), a grey key, the little-data and size keys, the line "Vergleichstage: n × weekday aus den 12 Wochen davor" and the meta line. The good/bad row of the period map is hidden. The labels and the panel name the day the data belongs to: while a new day loads, they keep the previous day's name.
- **Germany card.** A card above the legend names Germany, the day and its weekday, the nationwide value of the selected metric and its normal ("üblich x"). Its symbol follows the selected metric and is one of five hand-drawn weather symbols, from sun (much better than usual) through fair and cloudy to rain and thunderstorm (much worse), taken from the rank of the nationwide value among the reference days. Where the rank is missing (too few reference days, or a value the UI does not show) it is a dashed circle with the reason as its label. Symbols are never the only carrier: the card shows the values and each symbol has a text label.
- **Gap note and empty day.** A day with missing source hours is selectable; the card adds "Datenlücke: n h fehlen" and shows the neutral symbol labelled "Kein Vergleich möglich: Datenlücke an diesem Tag", never a rank. The stations keep their steps. A day with no stops in the selection (a gap day without data, or "Fernverkehr" on a day without long-distance stops) shows the card with "Keine Daten für diesen Tag" and no stations.
- **Tooltip and panel.** The tooltip names the value, the normal and the step word ("never colour only"), or "kein Vergleich möglich". In the station panel each tile has a stripe in the step colour and the line "üblich x · step" or "kein Vergleich möglich"; the footer reads "n = … · weekday, date".

**Normal.** The median of the same weekday over the previous 12 weeks, per metric, without days with missing hours, without nationwide public holidays and without days before the full-coverage month (when a day lies on or after it). At least 8 valid days must remain, otherwise there is no normal. A day is valid for a metric only where the UI would show its value (the same low-data rule as the trend). Days up to about 2025-12-27 therefore read "kein Vergleich möglich".

## 3 Decisions

- **A delay histogram per day, not counters.** The day table keeps (`day`, `gruppe`, `eva`, `bucket`, `n`), the same shape as the month aggregate without weekday and hour. Reason: all four metrics, filters by category and station, and the median and P90 can only be computed from a histogram (see [patterns.md](patterns.md) §3); counters would give the punctual share and cancellation rate only. It costs about 36 MB over 27 months (measured on 2025-06 and 2026-09).
- **The day key is the planned arrival date** (Europe/Berlin). Reason: the same rule as weekday and hour in the month aggregate, so a day's counts and the heatmap agree.
- **Edge days are summed across month files.** A file holds the stops planned on the edge day of its neighbour month (the month comes from `time`, see [pipeline.md](pipeline.md) §3), so the same day can sit in two files. `combine` sums per key instead of dropping rows. Reason: measured on the real files, the overlap is tiny (0 and 1 stops repeated at two month boundaries), and summing is correct whether a stop is repeated or split.
- **Day gaps use the same `GAP_SHARE` rule as the months.** `day_gaps` counts the thin hours of the month's hour table per calendar day, so the days add up to the month's `missing_hours`. Reason: one definition of "incomplete" for the month and the day views.
- **The trend is a centred 28-day median over days without gaps and with enough data, with at least 14 values.** A day with little data is one whose value the UI would not show (the low-data rule of [app-shell.md](app-shell.md) §2). Reason: a median ignores single bad days (strikes, storms) and a gap day would pull the line down for a reason that is not the railway; a trend drawn from days that the same page dashes as too thin would break the rule that no value is shown below the limit; with fewer than 14 values the line would claim a trend from too little.
- **Days are the default view, months a switch.** Reason: the daily series is the richer view and was the point of the feature; months stay for the long run and for readers who want the earlier chart.
- **Line series and the station sparkline stay monthly until the line page.** Reason: per-line days need per-run data that the line detail page brings; the day table has no line key.
- **A separate route `/tagesverlauf` instead of a parameter on `/zeitverlauf`.** Reason: the monthly contract, its models and its tests stay unchanged, and the daily series has fields the monthly one does not (gap hours, trend) and no `linie`.
- **For the day map, the normal is the median of the same weekday over the previous 12 weeks, without gap days and nationwide holidays, needing at least 8 valid days.** Reason: the same weekday removes the weekly rhythm, 12 weeks are recent enough to follow the timetable and long enough for a stable median, and holidays and gap days are not normal days. Fewer than 8 values would make the median depend on a few days.
- **The reference window starts no earlier than the full-coverage month.** Reason: before it about 131 stations report, after it about 5,100, so Germany's normal mixed two populations. Example: 2025-11-20 read 79.8 % against a "normal" of 73.9 %, a step that comes from the station set and not from the railway. Days up to about 2025-12-27 now have fewer than 8 references and read "kein Vergleich möglich", which is accurate. Station normals are per station and were not affected, but share the window, so the list of `referenztage` is what both rest on.
- **The day map shows the deviation from the normal on a five-step diverging scale.** Reason: the question of a day map is "worse than usual?", not "how punctual?", and a station that is always late must not look like a bad day.
- **"Better or worse than usual" is noise-aware: a station's step comes from z, its deviation from its normal divided by the spread of its own reference days.** The spread is 1.4826 times the median absolute deviation (a robust standard deviation) with a floor per metric (1 pp punctuality, 0.5 pp cancellation rate, 1 min median and P90); |z| below 2 is as usual, 2 up to 4 better or worse, 4 and more much better or much worse. Reason: fixed bounds of a few percentage points or minutes, the same for every station, sat below a small station's daily noise and coloured three quarters of the map on an ordinary day. Measured on 2026-09-08, -15 and -16: "as usual" went from 25 % to 75 to 84 % for punctuality and to 87 to 92 % for the median (§5). The product owner chose this.
- **The class is computed in the API, the frontend only maps it to a colour.** Reason: one rule, testable in pytest. A station has no class without a shown value or without 8 shown reference days.
- **The median floor is 1 min.** Reason: the stored medians are whole minutes, so a spread below one minute cannot be told from rounding. With a floor of half a minute "as usual" for the median was 72 to 78 % and the median leaned toward "worse".
- **The Germany symbol comes from the rank among the reference days and follows the selected metric.** Reason: the rank says how unusual the day is without a threshold on a metric that differs by season, and the card and the map then tell the same story. The product owner chose to follow the metric.
- **A gap day gets no Germany symbol.** Reason: the same one that keeps gap days out of the trend: a day with missing hours would be ranked against complete days, and the gap, not the railway, could be the reason. The neutral symbol names the gap. The product owner chose this; the stations keep their steps.
- **`days.parquet` has row groups of about one day (50,000 rows).** Reason: a one-day filter then skips the other groups. Without it, every distinct day queried grew the process, because Polars scanned all 24.5 M rows (§5). Statistics are Polars' default, the data is sorted by day, so a group covers about 1.7 days on average.
- **The cell statistics of `/tageskarte` are vectorised** (`cell_stats_frame` in `core/stats.py`, one sort and one group-by instead of a Python loop per cell). Reason: cold time (§5). `cell_stats` stays the definition: an equivalence test over 405 cells (`test_cell_stats_frame_equals_cell_stats`) compares both exactly.
- **The date field writes only complete days inside the data.** Reason: typing a year passed through `0002-09-15` and `0020-09-15`, which the clamp moved to the first data day mid-typing, each with a cold request. The field is uncontrolled and follows the URL only when the URL's day changes, because a controlled field reset its segments after every ignored half-typed value.
- **Labels follow the loaded day.** Reason: with the previous day's data still on screen during a day switch, the panel read "Di., 15.09.2026" next to Monday's values.
- **The URL key is `tag`** (`/karte?tag=YYYY-MM-DD`). Reason: the existing filter keys are German and short (`von`, `bis`, `gattung`), and a link to a day must be shareable.
- **Bundesländer come later.** Reason: the day map and the daily series are the new ground; a state layer on top needs its own design (weather symbols per state, then the stations).

## 4 Architecture and data keys

- `core/holidays.py`: `nationwide_holidays(year)` returns the nine nationwide dates as a `frozenset[date]`: New Year, Good Friday, Easter Monday, 1 May, Ascension, Whit Monday, 3 October and both Christmas days. Easter comes from the anonymous Gregorian algorithm, no library.
- `pipeline/aggregate.py`: `days` builds the per-day histogram of a month file, `day_gaps` the missing hours per calendar day; both reuse `_hour_counts`, which `meta` uses too and which calls `hour_table`, the one gap rule that the raw days use as well ([pipeline.md](pipeline.md) §3). `pipeline/update.py` lists them in `MONTH_FILES`; `combine` writes `data/days.parquet` and `data/tage.parquet` ([pipeline.md](pipeline.md) §2).
- `data/days.parquet`: `day`, `gruppe`, `eva`, `bucket`, `n`, sorted by day, EVA, group and bucket (NULL bucket = cancelled stop, never 0 minutes), written in row groups of `DAYS_ROW_GROUP` = 50,000 rows ([pipeline.md](pipeline.md) §2). `data/tage.parquet`: `day`, `missing_hours`, one row per calendar day up to the last complete day. Both include the raw days after the last monthly file.
- `backend/main.py`: `GET /tagesverlauf` (`eva`, `gattung`) returns `titel` and `tage` (`tag`, `n`, the four statistics, `fehlende_stunden`, `trend`); `trends` computes the centred median with `rolling_median_by`, and `tage_for` caches per selection and file modification times. `/datenstand` carries `erster_tag`, `letzter_tag` and `fehlende_stunden_tage`. The paths are `DAYS_PATH` and `TAGE_PATH`, both in the deploy download list ([patterns.md](patterns.md) §2 and §4, [deployment.md](deployment.md)).
- `core/normal.py` (pure, no framework imports): `reference_days(day, first, last, gap_days)` lists the same weekday of the previous 12 weeks, nearest first, without days outside the data, gap days and nationwide holidays. `normal(values)` is the median, `None` below 8 values. `weather(value, references, higher_is_better)` is the rank 1 (much better) to 5 (much worse): the share of references the value beats, ties counting half, cut into fifths, with integer arithmetic so no float edge case decides a step; `None` without a value or 8 references. An exact fifth boundary above the middle steps one toward 3, like the one below it, so the rank is symmetric.
  - `spread(values, floor)` is 1.4826 times the median absolute deviation from the median, at least `floor`, `None` below 8 values. `deviation_class(value, references, higher_is_better, floor)` is the step 1 to 5 from z (`USUAL_Z` 2, `MUCH_Z` 4); `None` without a value or a normal. `FLOORS` is the one table of the floors per metric, in the metric's own unit. `core/rankings.py` has `higher_is_better(metric)`, the one place that says only punctuality counts higher as better.
- `core/stats.py`: `cell_stats_frame(lf, keys)` computes the statistics of every (keys) cell of a (keys, bucket, n) frame in Polars: a running count of arrived stops per cell, then the smallest bucket with `cum >= q * arrived` as median and P90. It needs one row per (keys, bucket) and key columns without NULLs.
- `backend/main.py`: `GET /tageskarte` (`tag` as `YYYY-MM-DD`, `gattung`) returns the `Tageskarte`: `tag`, `referenztage` (nearest first), `fehlende_stunden`, `deutschland`, `n`, `ohne_koordinaten` and `stationen`. A malformed or impossible date is 422, a day outside `tage.parquet` 404.
  - `TageskartenStation` is the `/karte` station (`KartenStation`, built by the join `/karte` shares) plus `normal` and `klasse`. `Deutschland` is the day's four statistics plus `normal` and `wetter`. `Normal` holds `puenktlich`, `ausfallquote`, `median` and `p90`, each `None` without a normal; `Wetter` and `Klasse` hold the same four as the rank or step 1 to 5, or `None`. Only `puenktlich` counts higher as better.
  - `deutschland` is `None` (with `n` 0 and no stations) when the selection has no stops that day. Only stations with coordinates are listed; `ohne_koordinaten` counts the others. On a day with missing hours Germany's `wetter` is `None` for every metric; the stations' `klasse` stays.
  - A value feeds a normal, a rank or a step only where `shown(...)` holds, the rule the trend feed uses (at least 15 stops for the cancellation rate, enough arrived stops for the delay metrics). `counting` and `references_of` collect those values once per station and per nation. The day's own value gets a rank or a step only when it is shown too. A station's step needs 8 shown reference days.
  - The reference window starts at the first data day, or at the first day of the full-coverage month (`coverage_start()`) for a day on or after it. `monate.parquet`'s modification time is part of the cache key for that reason.
  - `tageskarte_for` reads `days.parquet` for the day and its reference days only (a `is_between` prefilter lets Polars skip row groups), groups to (day, station, bucket), and computes the statistics per (day, station) with `cell_stats_frame`; it is cached per process ([patterns.md](patterns.md) §8). The response is gzip-compressed.
- `frontend/src/DayWeather.tsx`: `WeatherCard` and the five symbols as SVG with token colours. `frontend/src/metrics.ts`: `tag` in `Filters`, `readFilters` and `writeFilters`; `API_FIELD` (metric to API field, the one map the trend and the rankings use too), `display`, `REFERENCE_WEEKS`, `figureText` and `deviation` (the API's step and the normal as text, `null` where there is nothing to compare). `frontend/src/MapPage.tsx` holds the second query, the day colouring, the legend and the panel lines; `frontend/src/Filters.tsx` the switch and the date field. A failed `/datenstand` shows the error banner in day mode instead of loading for ever.
- `frontend/src/ZeitverlaufPage.tsx`: the page with the Tag/Monat switch, the day and month rows and the table mode. `frontend/src/charts.tsx`: the chart pieces both resolutions share (`Plot`, `Line`, `TrendLine`, `Band`, `Markers`, `tipOf`). `frontend/src/metrics.ts`: `readFilters` and `writeFilters` carry `z`. The shell around it is in [app-shell.md](app-shell.md).
- Frontend keys: the daily series is the default of `/muster/zeitverlauf`, `z=monat` switches to months and is dropped on every other page; a line URL drops `z`, stays monthly and hides the switch; `tag` switches `/karte` to day mode.

## 8 Known shortcuts

- Regional public holidays (Epiphany, Corpus Christi and others) are left out of `core/holidays.py`. A regional holiday can therefore sit among the reference days of a normal.
- Day gaps are counted by the day of the poll time, while the day key of the histogram is the planned arrival. A gap around midnight can mark the neighbouring day.
- `tage_for` keeps the computed series per process (`lru_cache`, 16 entries, keyed by selection and both file modification times), like the other caches ([patterns.md](patterns.md) §8). The first request of a selection computes it: 1.6 s nationwide, 0.7 to 0.8 s for one station or `gattung=fern`, about 30 ms afterwards (measured, §5). An entry is one model per day. Revisit when the station page is used heavily or the days outgrow this.
- The nationwide trend around the start of full coverage (2025-11) blends two populations: about 135 stations before, all stations after, for roughly two weeks on each side of the change, because the window is centred. The "Datenbasis erweitert" marker explains the step; the monthly series has the same step. Not fixed. Resetting the rolling window at `vollabdeckung_ab` would remove it.
- `tageskarte_for` keeps the computed day per process (`lru_cache`, `maxsize=4`, keyed by the data files' modification times, category and day). An entry is a model with one object per station, about 10 MB of Python objects, hence only four. Twenty distinct days settle at a working set of about 420 MB (peak 580 MB), one `/karte` on top peaks at 685 MB, a cold day takes about 2.4 s (§5). Revisit when memory or cold time grows; the next lever is `shown`, `thin` and the normal in Polars, which would split the one rule.
- `days.parquet`'s row groups (`DAYS_ROW_GROUP`) are about one day only on average: 491 groups over 822 days, and 387 of them span more than one day, because early days have far fewer rows than the 30,000 average. A one-day filter reads one or two groups. A smaller constant is one line if memory asks for it.
- No test reads the row groups: neither Polars nor an installed library exposes them. A test with a hand-written footer reader was dropped; the evidence is the memory measurement in §5. If pyarrow becomes a dependency, a test of `pq.ParquetFile(...).metadata` is short.
- Nothing cross-checks that the API gives no class where the UI would call the value low: the API's `shown(...)` and the UI's `show()` are two implementations of the same rule, and the day map no longer asks `show()` before colouring. They agree while both keep that rule.
- On a screen about 720 px high the Germany card and the open legend cover a few dots at the western edge. A taller screen is fine; the period map's legend alone nearly did that already.
- At the end of the series the centred window shrinks to a trailing one over the last 14 days and still needs 14 values, so the trend reaches the newest day only while the last 14 days all feed it; every gap or thin day among them cuts one day off its tail. With the raw days and no gap in October it reaches the newest day. Allowing half the minimum in the windows that run past the last day would keep it there, at the price of a lagging trailing median.
- The trend is the median of the days that pass the low-data rule, per metric. A station whose days all have little data therefore has no trend line, although its days are still drawn.
