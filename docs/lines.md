# Line page

## 1 Overview

The page of one line (`/linie/<id>`): its stations along the route with the four statistics, the line's trend, and the "Zeitfresser", the sections between consecutive stations coloured by where trains lose or make up time, drawn on a map and listed as a strip. It is reached from a line row in the Rankings and in a station's line table. The Bildfahrplan (the graphic timetable of one day, planned against actual) comes next and is not built (§7). The page rests on two small month tables built from the stop number in each source row ([pipeline.md](pipeline.md) §2). The shell around it, the filter bar and the URL keys are in [app-shell.md](app-shell.md), the line names and ids in [pipeline.md](pipeline.md) §3.

## 2 Spec

**Route and keys.** `/linie/<id>`, the id in the path (`RE57:08000037`, percent-encoded). The keys are `k`, `von`, `bis` and `richtung` ([app-shell.md](app-shell.md) §2). Every other key (`g`, `bhf`, `tag`, `land`, `linie`, `z`) is dropped from the URL once, an invalid or default `richtung` too, so a shared link carries only what the page shows. Days in `von` and `bis` round outward to months. An unknown id is a state of the page, not an error: the heading "Diese Linie kennen wir nicht" and a link to the Rankings of lines. A failed request shows the error banner with a retry.

**Page.** From top to bottom:

1. **Header.** The breadcrumb "Rankings › label" (back to the line Rankings with `k`, `von` and `bis`, and `art=zuege` for a single train), the label as the heading, the train-type badge and "Teilen". A single train (`zug`) has the meta line "Einzelner Zug, keine Linie: wenige Fahrten je Bahnhof" under the heading. The filter bar has the Zeitraum only: no Bahnhof chip and no Gattung chip (a line has one type).
2. **Direction toggle.** "Richtung <end>" with the line's two end stations, key `richtung` (only `2` is written; direction 1, the axis order, is the default), under the filter bar and above the tiles. One toggle turns the station plan (5) and the Zeitfresser (7) together. It shows only when both directions have section values; a line that runs one way has no toggle, and the station plan and the strip both follow the direction that has data.
3. **Notes and tiles.** For a line without live data (`ohne_echtzeit`) the note "Für diese Linie liefert die Quelle keine Echtzeitdaten: alle Halte gelten als pünktlich." Then the four metric tiles of the station page (the one component `MetricTiles` in `frontend/src/ui.tsx`: value, sentence, n, coloured edge, the hatched look and the note for too little data) for the whole line, the stops of all stations pooled for the period (`gesamt`). Without `gesamt` the tiles show the empty look. The tiles, the stations and the Zeitfresser still show for a line without live data: they are what the data says, and the note explains them.
4. **Metric switch** with the four metrics, the same as on every page, key `k`. It changes the stations card only.
5. **Stations card.** The stations as a route plan: the axis from the start of the shown direction at the top to its end at the bottom, a vertical line on the left that connects a dot per station, larger dots at the first and the last station of the shown direction. A station the axis visits twice (ring, reversal) stands at each of its places. Below the route, outside it (no line, no dots), the heading "Weitere Halte" and the stations off the axis. A row: the name (a link to `/bahnhof/<eva>` keeping `k`, `von` and `bis`), a bar, the value of the selected metric, the number of stops and the cancellation rate. Stations with too little data show "–" hatched, by the rule of the other tables. On a phone a row is a card with the bar under the name.
6. **Trend.** The line's sparkline, linking to the Zeitverlauf of the line ([day-history.md](day-history.md) §2); the Zeitverlauf links back with "Linienseite →".
7. **Zeitfresser card**, in the direction of the toggle (2).
   - A legend of the five steps with their limits and the note "blass: weniger als 50 Fahrten".
   - **Map** (MapLibre, the same base map as the Karte): the axis sections of the chosen direction as solid segments in their step colour, every other section dashed. Hover shows "A → B: −0,8 min · 12.345 Fahrten".
   - **Strip**: one row per pair of consecutive axis stations in travel order, with the trips, a bar centred on zero and the signed minutes. A section without values reads "–" and "keine Werte" and adds nothing.
   - The line "Summe der Abschnitte: +1,1 min".
   - A closed group "Äste und Abschnitte mit ausgelassenen Halten (N)" lists the other sections, each with its own direction written out.
   - A line with no section values in the period says so and keeps the stations card.

The Zeitfresser have their own explanation line (minutes along the line, not a rate), because the metric switch does not apply to them.

**Colours.** The five steps use the diverging tokens `r1` to `r5` of the day map: green for minutes made up, red for minutes lost. Limits in minutes per section, provisional (§3): below −0,5, below −0,15, up to +0,15, up to +1,0, above. A section with fewer than 50 trips is drawn pale (50 % opacity); a missing section on the axis is a very pale grey segment. The strip carries the same colours, and the text always names the minutes, so the colour is never the only carrier.

**API.** Both routes take `linie`, `von` and `bis` (months); an unknown line is 404, a malformed parameter 422 (`linie` at most 200 characters).

- `GET /linie` returns `Linie`: `linie`, `name`, `label`, `typ`, `zug` and `ohne_echtzeit` (§3), `gesamt` (the four statistics of the whole line, all stations pooled for the period, null without stops), `achse` (the EVAs of the axis in order, a station may repeat) and `bahnhoefe`, a list of `LinienBahnhof` (`eva`, `name`, `lat`, `lon`, `pos`, `n`, `ausfallquote`, `median`, `p90`, `puenktlich`). `pos` is the station's first place on the axis from 0, null off it. A station with no stops in the period keeps `n` 0 and no statistics. The stations are the axis, the stations with stops of the line in the period and the ends of its sections, in the order `pos`, then the rest by stops and name; a station without a name in the source is left out.
- `GET /linie/abschnitte` returns a list of `Abschnitt`: `von_eva`, `nach_eva`, `n` and `minuten` (the mean, two decimals), only sections with trips, sorted by the two EVAs.

## 3 Decisions

- **A trip is the `id` without its last part.** Every source row has `id` = `{ride id}-{YYMMDDHHmm}-{stop number}`, so the rest identifies one trip on one day and the last part is the stop order. Reason: the source has no trip column, and `train_line_station_num` does not say which trip a stop belongs to. The stop number is the column `train_line_station_num`, else the last part of `id`, so the raw days (which have no column) work too.
- **A section is between two consecutive stop numbers of one trip.** Reason: 4.4 % of trips skip a stop number (measured on 2026-10, 14.8 million rail rows); pairing a stop with the next one present would show a long section for a skipped stop and attribute the delay of two sections to one. A gap makes no section.
- **The change is the delay at the end minus the delay at the start, in whole minutes.** A stop's delay is its arrival delay, or its departure delay at a first stop (which has no planned arrival), by the delay rules of [pipeline.md](pipeline.md) §3. A section is left out when either end is cancelled, implausible or has no planned time. Reason: a cancelled stop has no delay, and cancellations stay in the stations card and never count as 0 minutes. A stop with no change time counts as on time, like everywhere else. The cleaning (duplicates, implausible delays) runs first, the same as for every other table.
- **The measure is the mean minutes gained or lost per section, and the line under the strip is called "Summe der Abschnitte".** The mean is the sum of the changes over the trips through the section, so the means of the sections along a line add up. The sum is not the delay growth of one trip, because every section counts all trips that run through it, including the ones that start or end inside the line. Real data, RE57 Dortmund Hbf – Warburg (Westf), October 2026 (18 axis stations, 17 sections): the API's section means equal an independent recomputation from the source file (n differs by at most 2 per section, the cleaning's duplicate rule); the sections add up to +1,63 min, while the 204 trips that ran the whole way gain +0,62 min on average. One section alone makes up 5,3 min (`08000054` to `08002992`). Reason for the mean over the median or a rate: only a mean adds up along the line, and the product owner asked for sections that do.
- **The page uses whole months of the shared Zeitraum, like the other month views; the current month's raw days are left out.** Reason: the section tables are built per month file ([pipeline.md](pipeline.md) §2), and a partial month would skew them, as it does on the Rankings.
- **The axis of a line is its stop pattern with the most station visits (trips times stops), from the newest month in which the line has routes.** Reason: lines branch a lot (RE57 has 60 stop patterns and 39 pairs of end stations, mostly short turns); the visits weight lets the long main route beat many short turns. Ties go to the lower pattern, so the result is stable. The stations off the axis (branches) follow in their own group and on the map. See §8 for the newest-month shortcut.
- **An axis may visit a station twice, and the page follows `achse`, not `pos`.** A ring (the Berlin S41 and S42 start and end at Beusselstraße) or a pattern that reverses at a spur (RE14 at Roßlau) repeats a station on its axis; 147 lines do. `achse` keeps every visit in order, and the strip's pairs come from it, so the ring's closing section and the reversal's sections stay in the strip and the sum. A station keeps its first `pos` for the stations card. On a ring both toggle buttons name the same station. Reason: one `pos` per station cannot hold a second visit, and the first version dropped the closing section and showed a section that does not exist.
- **A section without values reads "keine Werte", not "keine Fahrten".** A section is measured between consecutive stop numbers only; when a stop between two axis stations has no rows in the data (S5 Berlin-Kaulsdorf to Berlin-Biesdorf), trains did run but the pair has no section. The rule stays (a gap never fakes a section); only the claim was wrong.
- **The direction follows the axis order: the toggle names the line's two end stations, direction 1 runs along the axis, 2 against it, and one toggle in the page header turns the station plan and the Zeitfresser together.** Reason: a line has no other natural direction, and one direction at a time keeps the strip a single journey and the route plan one journey that reads from top to bottom. Only `richtung=2` is written to the URL, so the default link stays short. A line with section values in one direction only has no toggle, and the station plan and the strip show that direction (the URL key is ignored then).
- **The axis is turned to the label's order, in `combine`.** The most travelled pattern may run either way (IC34 "Dortmund Hbf – Dillenburg" ran Dillenburg to Dortmund), and the page wants the label's start at the top. `core.sections.orient_axis` reverses an axis whose last station is the line's first end station or whose first station is its second end station, and keeps one that already starts at the first end (so a ring that starts at it is not turned); the rest stays as it is. The match is by name, both sides through the destination spelling fix, because the end stations are destination strings and not EVAs (shortcut, §8). The ends are the label's two end stations (`core.lines.top_ends`, the one place that ranks them). The API needs no change: `achse` arrives oriented.
- **The station plan is a route, not a table.** Reason: a line is a journey, and a vertical line with dots reads as one at a glance; the stations off the axis are not on that journey, so they sit below it without line and dots, under their own heading. The line and dot colours are the theme tokens `text-2` and `text-3`, which have a light and a dark set.
- **Branches and sections that skip stops are drawn dashed, with both directions pooled.** A section that is not between two consecutive axis stations has no direction of travel on the axis, so the map shows one dashed segment per pair of stations with the mean of both directions weighted by trips, and the tooltip then reads "A ↔ B". The list below the strip keeps each direction apart. Reason: a branch drawn twice on top of itself would hide one direction. A section to a station without coordinates is not drawn.
- **The colour limits are provisional: below −0,5, below −0,15, up to +0,15, up to +1,0, above, in minutes per section (`STEPS` in `frontend/src/sections.ts`, the one place).** Measured on 2026-10, over the sections with n of at least 100, the 1, 5, 25, 50, 75, 95 and 99 % points of the section mean are −1,54, −0,69, −0,15, 0,14, 0,51, 1,34 and 2,59 minutes, so the limits sit near the 25th and 50th percentile, between the 5th and the 25th, and between the 75th and the 95th. The product owner confirms them in the test round.
- **A section with fewer than 50 trips is pale.** Reason: the same cut-off as the "~" tier of the map's dots, so little data never draws the eye.
- **`/linie/<id>` is the page of a line, and line rows in the Rankings and in the station's line table open it, keeping `k`, `von` and `bis`.** The rows used to open the Zeitverlauf of the line; that is now one click further, from the sparkline. Reason: a line has a page of its own with the question "where does it lose time", and the series stays reachable from it. The category is not kept, since a line has one type. The id is the path, not a query key: a page of its own should have its own address.
- **The routes take the line as a query parameter (`/linie?linie=`), like `/zeitverlauf`.** Reason: an id holds spaces and a colon, which a query value carries without further rules.
- **Gattung is dropped on this page, and with it the Bahnhof chip.** Reason: a line has one type, and the station is the line's own.
- **The stations card shows stops of the line in the period for every metric, all categories summed.** It reads `lines.parquet` through the same cell statistics as the station's line table, so a station's value here equals its row there ([patterns.md](patterns.md)).
- **Whole-line tiles at the top of the page.** Most line pages looked thin: 1,199 of the 2,071 lines in the Rankings (58 %) are single train numbers such as "ICE 2591 → München Hbf" that run about 34 times a year, so each station has 30 to 34 stops. The four tiles pool the stops of every station of the line for the period (`gesamt`, the same cell statistics as a station row, so the stops are pooled and not the station values averaged), which gives the page numbers with some weight. Reason: the page answers "how reliable is this line" before it goes into stations.
- **A line without live data is marked, and left out of the Rankings.** Five lines topped the "best" list with 100 % because the source holds no real-time data for them, so every stop reads as exactly on time. The rule, in `core.lines.no_live_data`: at least `MIN_STOPS` = 500 stops over all months and at least `NO_LIVE_PERCENT` = 99 % of the arrived stops exactly on time, in integer arithmetic; a cancelled stop is not an arrived one and never counts as 0 minutes. The page says so in a note and still shows the tiles, stations and Zeitfresser, since they are what the data says. The Rankings leave such lines out in both lists. Reason: a 100 % that comes from missing data is not a result, and the lowest 500 stops keep a short quiet line from being marked by chance.
- **Lines and single trains rank apart.** `zug` is set when the name is a train type and a number alone (`core.lines.is_train_number`, `^\S+ \d+$`): "ICE 2591", "RE 57", "S-Bahn 1" survive the space and count, "S 1 x" does not. The Rankings of lines have a switch Linien · Einzelzüge (key `art`, default Linien, URL key only for `zuege`, `art` in `/rankings`), and the single trains are their own list. Reason: a single train and a line with a timetable are different things to compare, and 58 % of the entries drowned the lines. The header of a single train says so, so its thin station rows are expected.
- **The two routes are not cached.** Measured with the test client on the real data, one month: `/linie` cold 0,29 s, warm 0,04 s; `/linie/abschnitte` 0,02 s. Reason: the line filter is pushed into the scans and the files are small.

## 4 Architecture and data keys

- `core/sections.py` (pure, no framework imports): `trip_key_expr`, `stop_number_expr`, `stop_delay_expr` (the delay a stop hands to the next section), `section_changes` (one row per section between consecutive stop numbers, with the change) `line_axis` (per line the pattern with the most visits, as `linie`, `pos`, `eva`) and `orient_axis` (turns it to the label's order, §3).
- `pipeline/aggregate.py`: `sections` builds `sections.parquet` of a month and `routes` builds `routes.parquet`, both through `_trip_stops`. `pipeline/update.py`: `combine` calls `write_line_sections`, which writes the two combined files for the lines of `linien.parquet` ([pipeline.md](pipeline.md) §2). A section finds its line by line name and its first station; a pattern the same way by its first station.
- `data/abschnitte.parquet`: `linie`, `from_eva`, `to_eva`, `month`, `n`, `minutes` (the sum of the changes), sorted by line, month and the two EVAs. `data/linienweg.parquet`: `linie`, `pos`, `eva`, the axis. Real sizes with the months to 2026-10: 2,9 MB and 0,24 MB; one month adds about 47,000 section rows and 23,700 route rows, 0,6 MB.
- `backend/main.py`: `ABSCHNITTE_PATH` and `LINIENWEG_PATH` (defaults `data/<name>.parquet`), both in `DATA_FILES`, so the deployment downloads them ([deployment.md](deployment.md) §4). `known_line` is the one 404 check (`/zeitverlauf` uses it too), `line_sections` sums the sections over the period, `line_page` and `line_abschnitte` are the routes. The reads go through `collect`, the cap on concurrent queries.
- `frontend/src/LinePage.tsx`: the page, with the toggle in the header, the route plan (`Stops`, `RouteMark`), the legend and the strip. `frontend/src/LineMap.tsx`: the small map; `data-testid="line-map"`, `data-ready` once drawn and `data-segments` (the drawn segments as JSON) for the tests. `frontend/src/sections.ts`: `STEPS`, `THIN_N`, `stepOf`, `minutesText`, `axisStations` (the stations along `achse`, repeats kept) and `cutLine` (from `achse`: rows, sum, whether any row has a section, the other sections and the map's segments), all pure. The base map of this page and the Karte is one function, `baseMap` in `frontend/src/MapPage.tsx` (with `themeColours`), and a station row here and in the station's line table is one component, `StatRow` in `frontend/src/ui.tsx` (with `RowHead`; its `marker` prop carries the route dot).
- URL: `linePath(id)` in `frontend/src/ui.tsx`, the route `/linie/:id` in `frontend/src/App.tsx`. `richtung` is a `Filters` key in `frontend/src/metrics.ts`, written after `z` and before `k`; `linePageSearch` builds the search of a link to the page. `Filters` in `frontend/src/Filters.tsx` takes `noCategory`.
- `frontend/src/ui.tsx`: `MetricTiles`, shared with the station page. `frontend/src/RankingsPage.tsx`: the second switch; `Rank.art` in `frontend/src/metrics.ts` (`readRank`, `writeRank`).
- `core/lines.py`: `is_train_number`, `no_live_data`, `MIN_STOPS`, `NO_LIVE_PERCENT`; `pipeline/update.py` `combine` writes `zug` and `ohne_echtzeit` into `linien.parquet`; `backend/main.py` `/rankings` (`art`) and `/linie` (`zug`, `ohne_echtzeit`, `gesamt`).
- Catalog: the keys `line.*` and `rk.art.*`, `rk.artLabel`, `rk.meta.zuege` in both languages.

## 8 Known shortcuts

- The axis comes from the newest month in which the line has routes (`ponytail:` comment in `write_line_sections`, `pipeline/update.py`). A route that changed inside the period is drawn on its newest form; a section of the older route then lands among the branches. Revisit if a changed route shows up in a long period.
- A branch has no direction, so the map pools both directions with the mean weighted by trips (`ponytail:` comment in `segmentsOf`, `frontend/src/sections.ts`). Revisit if the direction of a branch matters.
- The axis orientation matches end stations to station names (`ponytail:` comment in `orient_axis`, `core/sections.py`). A line whose end station is spelled differently from its station name keeps the direction of its most travelled pattern. Revisit if many lines show the wrong way round; the fix is to map destinations to EVAs.
- The section tables hold whole months only; the current month's raw days are not in them.
- The hover text of a map segment has no end-to-end test: the map canvas has no handle in the tests, so its text is covered through `data-segments` and the strip, which says the same.
